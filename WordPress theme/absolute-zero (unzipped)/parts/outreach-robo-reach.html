Robo Reach

A score-prediction tool we built for every FIRST Tech Challenge team, not just ours. Enter a team number and it will tell you how your matches are likely to go, and keep telling you while the event is running. It is free, it needs no account, and it works for any of the roughly fifteen thousand registered FTC teams worldwide.

What it tells you

Four things, in the order the app puts them in front of you.

  • Any team, any event

    Type a team number. You get that team’s season power ratings, its world rank, and every event it is registered for.

  • A predicted score per match

    Both alliance scores and a win probability for every match on the schedule, sitting next to the actual score once the match is played.

  • Who else is in the room

    Strength rankings for every team attending the event, so you know what you are up against before the schedule comes out.

  • Live during the event

    The event page refreshes every thirty seconds, so scores and predictions stay current while you are still in the pit.

How the prediction works

Two models, switchable on any event page, both backtested against real events rather than against themselves.

Smart

Learned, EPA-style

It pulls every played match this season involving any team at your event, plus every match their past partners and opponents played. That is typically three to twelve thousand matches. Each team starts at its season OPR, then the model replays all of it in order on penalty-free scores, shifting ratings toward what actually happened. The replay runs three times so early matches are judged with converged ratings.

OPR sum

Baseline

Straight addition of both teams’ season OPRs plus the average penalty bonus, with its own calibrated win probabilities. It is there so you can see what the learned model is actually buying you.

What it scored in testing

Across three events, a qualifier and two regional championships, the learned model called 75 to 78 percent of winners with an average score error of 21 to 29 points. The naive version it replaced managed 64 to 73 percent, with 24 to 33 points of error. Both numbers are on the page because a prediction tool that will not show you its own error rate is asking for trust it has not earned.

Ratings marked with an asterisk in the app are provisional: fewer than three played matches.

Where the numbers come from

Teams, events, schedules, detailed scores and OPRs come from the FTCScout API, which is free and open. The prediction model trains on that.

During a live event the app also reads the official FIRST Tech Challenge events API, so a match that has just been scored shows up within seconds instead of waiting for a sync. Those requests go through a small server function of ours that holds the credentials, so no key is ever shipped to a visitor’s browser. If it is unavailable the app falls back to FTCScout without complaining.

Nothing about you is collected, and there is nothing to sign in to.

Why this is on our outreach page

Scouting is the part of a competition where a well-resourced team pulls furthest ahead of a rookie one. A team with eight spare students can watch every match and keep a spreadsheet. A team with four cannot.

Robo Reach closes some of that gap for free, for anybody, including the teams we compete against. That is the same reason we publish our notebooks and run the Collaboration Summit, and it is the part of FIRST we actually care about.
