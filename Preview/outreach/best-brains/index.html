Best Brains

Introductory robotics and coding sessions at Best Brains learning centres across Loudoun County, pitched at the eight to fourteen age group. For most of the room it is the first time they have heard the word FIRST.

Ask about a session
Ages
8 to 14
Centres

Format
Short sessions, run in term

Why this age group specifically.

Best Brains works with children from three to fourteen. That ceiling is exactly where FIRST LEGO League ends and FIRST Tech Challenge begins, which makes their older cohort the single best-timed audience we have. A twelve year old who meets a competition robot in a Best Brains classroom has two clear years to join a team before high school makes the decision for them.

Children at a Best Brains centre programming a small robot on a table. A Best Brains session: children at a table with a small robot and laptops.
3 to 14

The age range Best Brains serves. The top of it is where FIRST Tech Challenge starts.

Block coding first

A drivable robot and a block editor. Sequence, loop, condition, and then a robot that reacts to a sensor rather than repeating a script.

A build with a fixed parts budget

Constraint is the lesson. A fixed budget forces a trade, and a trade forces a conversation about what the robot is actually for.

A completed student-built robot on a classroom table beside its parts kit. A finished student build at a Best Brains session, kit visible.

The route in

Every session ends the same way: this is FIRST LEGO League, this is FIRST Tech Challenge, here is when the season opens and here is how to find a team. Named, dated, written down.

How this fits the mission

FIRST asks its teams to grow the programme. The honest way to do that is not to talk about robotics in general to whoever is available. It is to find the rooms where children of exactly the right age are already sitting down to learn something, and to walk into those rooms with a real robot and a specific invitation.

Best Brains is one of those rooms. Curie Learning is another. The libraries are a third. The material changes slightly; the last five minutes never do.
