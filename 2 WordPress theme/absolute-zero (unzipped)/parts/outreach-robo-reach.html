Robo Reach

Our travelling workshop. We load a competition robot, a driver station and a build table into a car and take them to a library, a school or a community room. Every child in the room drives the robot. Nobody watches from the back.

How a session runs

It opens with the robot moving, because nothing we could say first would compete with that. Then the room splits: half drive, half build, and they swap. The build challenge is timed and scored, which converts a craft activity into an engineering one, and it is the part children argue about on the way out.

The last five minutes are the ones that matter. We name the FIRST programme that fits the age group in the room, we say when the season starts, and we tell families where to find a team near them. A workshop that ends without that is entertainment.

What comes out of the car

Four things, and the fourth is the one that has a lasting effect.

  • The competition robot

    The actual machine that competed this season, with a driver station and a scoring element. Not a demo bot.

  • A mechanism, in pieces

    One subsystem taken off the robot and taken apart, so a child can hold the gear that makes the arm move.

  • Build kits

    Enough kits for the room, and a timed challenge that everybody can finish.

  • The route in

    Which FIRST programme fits the age group, when the season starts, and where the nearest team is.

Hosting one

We need a room, tables, a power outlet and about ninety minutes. A hard floor is better than carpet for driving. We handle everything else, including the kits, and there is no charge at any point.
